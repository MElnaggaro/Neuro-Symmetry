Neuro-Symmetry

Every 40 seconds, someone in the world has a stroke. The warning signs appear on the face — milliseconds before irreversible damage begins. We built the engine that sees them first.

The problem
0.0M
neurons lost · every minute · untreated stroke Time is Brain

The thrombolysis window closes in 4.5 hours. Every minute of delay costs function that never returns — speech, movement, cognition, life.

The tragedy: the first signs manifest on the face — facial droop, asymmetric motion, a subtle deviation — minutes before other symptoms appear. Yet no one in the room is trained to measure them in real time.

Market Gap · Egypt

The system fails before the patient arrives.

Delayed Emergency Response

Cairo's average ambulance response time exceeds 45 minutes. In rural governorates, it can surpass 90. The thrombolysis window is already closing — before the first call is made.

Avg. response · 45–90 min

Centralized Stroke Care

Over 70% of Egypt's certified stroke units are concentrated in Cairo and Alexandria. Patients in Upper Egypt, Sinai, and the Delta have no access to specialist intervention within the golden hour.

Coverage · <30% of population

Zero Early-Warning Tools

No technology exists to detect stroke warning signs at the point of first contact — the home, the rural clinic, the community pharmacy. Critical minutes pass unrecognized and unmeasured.

First-contact tools · none
The insight

Faces betray neurology before words do.

A millimeter of droop. A fraction of a second of asymmetric motion. With dense facial landmarks and learned features, these deviations become measurable in real time — without specialized hardware, without a clinician in the room.

The solution

The bridge Egypt doesn't have — built from a webcam.

Zero Hardware Cost

Runs on any laptop, tablet, or smartphone camera already in a clinic or home. No capital expenditure, no supply chain, no installation — deployable in any of Egypt's 27 governorates today.

Commodity hardware · Day-one ready

First-Minutes Alert

Detects asymmetry before the ambulance is called. In a city where traffic costs 40 minutes, those extra minutes aren't a luxury — they are the difference between full recovery and permanent disability.

~33 ms latency · 30+ FPS

A Bridge, Not a Diagnosis

Not a replacement for a stroke unit. A trigger — "The face says go." Designed to integrate with Egypt's emergency networks and guide families to act before the window closes.

Assistive · XAI explainable
